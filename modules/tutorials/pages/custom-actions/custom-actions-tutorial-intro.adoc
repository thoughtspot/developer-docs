Use custom actions to send the data a user clicks on to your app or an external URL, so they can act on it without leaving your portal.

== The scenario

Imagine you're a full-stack developer at SpotStay, a short-term rental company. Your team runs an internal portal that hosts use every day to check bookings, occupancy, and maintenance status across their listings, through an embedded ThoughtSpot Liveboard.

Hosts can now see when occupancy drops. What they can't do is tell anyone without leaving the portal: they copy the number, switch to Slack, and explain what they saw.

You'll add a *Flag to team* menu item to the charts on the bookings Liveboard. When a host clicks it, your app receives the data for the point they clicked and posts a message to the team's Slack channel.

Custom actions are always user-triggered. The host decides what's worth flagging; the action removes the copy-and-paste. If you want the team notified automatically when a KPI crosses a threshold, use link:https://docs.thoughtspot.com/cloud/latest/monitor[KPI alerts, window=_blank] instead.

Follow along in code with the link:https://github.com/nrentz-ts/spottercode[SpotStay app on GitHub, window=_blank].

[#before-you-begin]
== Before you begin

Make sure you have the following:

* Developer or Admin privilege on your ThoughtSpot instance.
* Visual Embed SDK v1.43.0 or later, the version that introduced `CustomActionTarget` and `CustomActionsPosition`.
* A working Liveboard embed. To embed a Liveboard in the SpotStay app, follow xref:build-embed-app-with-spottercode-intro.adoc[Embed ThoughtSpot with SpotterCode].
* A working backend. Your frontend sends the flag to a backend route that forwards it. This example uses Slack, but you can send it anywhere your team works.
